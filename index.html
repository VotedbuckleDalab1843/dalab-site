About the Lab

The Diversity in Adolescence Lab is a research lab in the Department of Human Development and Family Studies at Michigan State University. It is directed by Dr. Yijie Wang and engages undergraduate and graduate students interested in adolescent development within diverse contexts.

We conduct research on adolescent development in the psychological, physical, and cognitive domains, with a particular interest in cultural and ethnic/racial diversity. Our projects examine socioemotional well-being, academics, health behaviors, sleep, stress responses, and executive functioning.

We seek to understand how these outcomes are shaped by family, peer, school, and neighborhood environments, and we are especially interested in the opportunities and challenges that come with diversity, such as ethnic/racial discrimination, socialization, and identity development.

Director

YW

Yijie Wang, Ph.D.

Director · Associate Professor, Human Development and Family Studies

Dr. Wang earned her B.A. in Psychology at Peking University and her Ph.D. in Human Development and Family Sciences at the University of Texas at Austin. Her research focuses on adolescent development in the context of race, ethnicity, immigration, and social inequality.

She studies socio-cultural factors (such as ethnic/racial socialization), bio-behavioral factors (such as sleep), and cognitive factors (such as executive function) that can help adolescents thrive despite racism and inequality. Her studies use longitudinal and daily diary designs to understand both short-term and long-term developmental mechanisms.

Her work has been funded by the National Institutes of Health and the William T. Grant Foundation, and it appears in Child Development, Developmental Psychology, Journal of Adolescent Health, and JAMA. She received the APS Rising Star Award in 2019 and the APA Distinguished Scientific Award for an Early Career Contribution to Psychology in 2024.

Postdoctoral Trainee

YZ

Youchuan Zhang, Ph.D.

Postdoctoral Research Fellow

Dr. Zhang holds a Bachelor of Medicine from Sun Yat-sen University, a Master of Social Science in Psychology from the University of Hong Kong, and a Ph.D. in Human Development and Family Studies from Michigan State University.

His research focuses on disadvantaged youth, especially ethnic-racial minority adolescents in the U.S. and adolescents from low-income families in rural China. He examines how social contexts, particularly structural and neighborhood conditions, shape psychosocial and bio-behavioral development.

His methods include daily diaries, longitudinal models, salivary sampling, and actigraphy-based sleep assessment, analyzed with multilevel and dynamic structural equation modeling and latent growth modeling.

Jiayi Liu

Doctoral Student · Diversity, Youth, and Family Development (DYAD)

Jiayi studies the cultural beliefs, values, and practices behind everyday family interactions, with a focus on how parenting affects youth well-being in changing cultural contexts such as modernization and social stratification. The goal is to inform clinical practice and policy through a sociocultural lens.

Using mixed methods, Jiayi is studying Chinese international students’ views of their parents and the role of parental support in acculturation, as well as microaggressions and ethnic-racial socialization in adoptive families in the U.S. Outside of research, Jiayi enjoys traveling, cooking, and board games.

Research Projects

Discrimination & Substance Use Project

Investigates the influence of discrimination on adolescents’ substance use outcomes using data from the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) Study.

Funding: National Institute on Minority Health and Health Disparities

Peers and Diversity (PAD) Project

Investigates how ethnic/racial and cultural processes in peer groups influence adolescents’ cognitive functioning, psychological well-being, academics, and sleep. The project follows 9th graders in high school, collecting daily diary and sleep actigraphy data annually.

Funding: William T. Grant Foundation; MSU Social Science College Faculty Initiatives Fund

Peers in Rural China Project

Investigates how peer relationships influence early adolescents’ psychological well-being, sleep, and cortisol output in rural China. The project sampled 7th and 8th graders in a rural boarding school, collecting daily diary and salivary cortisol data.

Congruence & Social Norm Project

Investigates adolescents’ substance use and risky sexual behaviors in diverse ethnic groups using nationally representative data from the National Longitudinal Study of Adolescent to Adult Health.

Funding: National Institute on Alcohol Abuse and Alcoholism
